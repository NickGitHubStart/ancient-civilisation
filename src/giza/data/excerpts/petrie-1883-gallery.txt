The holes cut in the ramps or benches, along the sides of the gallery […] are as yet inexplicable. One remarkable point is that the holes are alternately long and short, on both sides of the gallery; the mean of the long holes is 23.32 […] and the mean of the short holes is 20.51 […]. Thus the horizontal length of a long hole is equal to the sloping length of a short hole, both being one cubit.

[…] mean of all, 20.55, for the sum of the seven projections of the laps, or one cubit, the laps being then one palm each in breadth.

— W. M. Flinders Petrie, The Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh (1883), ch. 7 §§45–46
